## The problem

Alaska has far more terrain than it has weather stations. Precipitation is
measured at scattered points, but the quantity you actually want is the value
everywhere in between — a smooth surface fitted to a sparse, noisy scatter of
readings.

## Approach

**Monomials first, to show why they fail.** Fitting powers of x to a noisy
one-dimensional signal captures the rough shape but misses the crests, and the
two curves diverge as soon as you look past the edges of the data. Every
monomial is global: each one is pulled on by every measurement at once, and
powers of x blow up as x grows.

**Radial basis functions instead.** An RBF is a bell curve centred at a point,
with a width parameter setting how far its influence reaches. Because it decays
to nearly zero away from its centre, each basis element fits one local stretch
of the data instead of all of it. The model is still a *linear* combination of
nonlinear functions, so finding the coefficients is still a least-squares
problem — the locality is free.

**Least squares by hand.** No solver shortcuts. The regressor matrix is
assembled row by row, one row per measurement, and the overdetermined system is
solved through an LU factorisation with permutations followed by forward and
backward substitution. The result is the coefficient vector minimising the
squared norm of the residual.

**From a curve to a surface.** The 2D case is the same algebra with wider
vectors: each centre is a location on the map rather than a single x value, and
the distance in the RBF exponent becomes the distance between two vectors. The
regressor matrix and the LU pipeline carry over unchanged, and what comes out
is a precipitation surface over Alaska.

**Choosing how many bases.** The number of RBFs is the real design knob. Too
few and the surface underfits; too many and the centres crowd together, the
columns of the regressor matrix stop being linearly independent, and the
factorisation collapses into near-zero pivots. Picking it is a trade between
fitting error and a system that can actually be solved.
