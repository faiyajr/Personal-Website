## The problem

An offshore wind farm needs wind that's strong but not destructive, and waves
low enough to build and maintain in. Deciding on a site means checking one
location against several datasets at once.

## Approach

**Five constraints.** A site has to pass all of these:
1. Average wind speed within the turbines' working range.
2. Average wave height under a safe maximum.
3. Waves under that maximum for enough of the time, measured from the buoy's
   readings.
4. Every rogue wave staying below the platform deck.
5. Wave heights steady enough that their spread stays small relative to the
   average.

**Global versus local.** Global model averages suggest where to look, and the
buoy's hourly measurements show what actually happens at the site. The
analysis uses both and compares them directly.

**One figure.** The results are plotted together: global wind and wave maps,
the candidate-site mask with the chosen location marked, a histogram of buoy
wave heights, and the buoy readings against the global average over time.
